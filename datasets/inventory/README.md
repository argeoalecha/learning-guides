# Inventory

Used by: **[Data Analyst guide](../../data-analyst/da_learning_guide.html)** (Phase 3 Capstone, Phase 4 Capstone, Final Capstone)

Synthetic warehouse-management dataset that pairs with [`superstore-sales/`](../superstore-sales/) — every row joins back to it on **Product ID**. It exists because the guide's later capstones layer inventory analytics (reorder alerts, dead-stock detection, running stock balances) on top of the sales data, and demand needed to be modeled independently of the sales sample (2,000 orders spread across 1,720 products is too sparse to derive believable per-product demand from directly).

Demand, reorder points, and stock levels were generated by simulating a simple **(s, S) reorder policy** week-by-week across the same 2023-01-01 → 2025-12-31 span as `superstore-sales`: each product consumes Poisson-distributed weekly demand, and once its stock drops to or below its reorder point, a purchase order is placed and arrives after that product's supplier lead time. This is what produces a realistic mix of healthy stock, ~19% of products currently sitting below their reorder point (the alert exercise), and slow movers with old `Last Restock Date` values (the dead-stock exercise) — instead of hand-picking which rows should trigger alerts.

## Files

| File | Use |
|---|---|
| `inventory_stock.csv` | One row per product (1,720 rows) — current stock, reorder point, safety stock, lead time, supplier, unit cost |
| `inventory_receipts.csv` | Purchase-order receiving log (~31.7k rows) — every restock event with date, quantity, and cost |
| `inventory_suppliers.csv` | 15 suppliers — lead time and reliability rating, referenced by `inventory_stock.Supplier ID` |

## Columns

**`inventory_stock.csv`**
`Product ID, Product Name, Category, Sub-Category, Warehouse, Supplier ID, Unit Cost, Lead Time Days, Avg Daily Demand, Safety Stock, Reorder Point, Beginning Stock, Current Stock, Last Restock Date`

**`inventory_receipts.csv`**
`Receipt ID, Product ID, Supplier ID, Receipt Date, Quantity Received, Unit Cost`

**`inventory_suppliers.csv`**
`Supplier ID, Supplier Name, Country, Avg Lead Time Days, Reliability Rating`

## Notes for exercises

- Join key throughout is **Product ID**, matching `superstore-sales/dim_Product.csv` and `fact_Sales.csv` — Product Name/Category/Sub-Category are duplicated on `inventory_stock` so it also works as a flat table on its own.
- **Reorder alert** (`current stock < reorder point`): true for ~19% of products in this snapshot — a meaningful minority, not all-or-nothing.
- **Running inventory balance** (`start stock + receipts - shipments`): use `Beginning Stock` as the starting point, `inventory_receipts` for receipts, and `superstore-sales` order `Quantity` (grouped by Product ID) for shipments.
- **Dead stock**: cross-reference `Last Restock Date` / low `Avg Daily Demand` here against `superstore-sales` order history — products with no *orders* in the last 60 days come from the sales side, not this table.
- `Beginning Stock` and `Current Stock` are snapshots at the start and end of the 3-year span respectively, not derived from actual `superstore-sales` shipment volume (see rationale above) — treat the two datasets as sharing a product catalog, not a reconciled ledger.

## Regenerating

Generated by the same script as `superstore-sales`, `shopsphere`, `ecommerce-orders`, and `shopflow` — see the root [`datasets/README.md`](../README.md). Deterministic (seeded RNG, `np.random.default_rng(42)`); re-running `gen_datasets.py` reproduces this data byte-for-byte, provided `superstore-sales` is regenerated in the same run (inventory depends on its product catalog).
