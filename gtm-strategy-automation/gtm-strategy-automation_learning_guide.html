The Four GTM Motions

Every GTM plan is built around one dominant motion. Choosing the wrong one wastes budget and misaligns the team.

  • Product-Led Growth (PLG): Product is the acquisition channel. Users self-onboard, hit a value moment, and convert or upgrade. Works when time-to-value is under 10 minutes. Examples: Slack, Figma, Notion.
  • Sales-Led Growth (SLG): Reps drive pipeline via outbound or inbound. Required when the buyer is not the user, the deal involves procurement, or customization is needed. Examples: Salesforce, Workday.
  • Marketing-Led Growth: Content, SEO, and brand create demand that funnels to sales or self-serve. Works for complex topics where buyers research extensively. Examples: HubSpot early days, Semrush.
  • Community-Led Growth: A network of practitioners creates demand and trust before the first sales touch. Works when the problem is cross-company (e.g., devtools, data stacks). Examples: dbt, Figma community.

GTM Motion Selection Framework

GTM MOTION SELECTION MATRIX

Dimension               PLG         SLG         Mktg-Led    Community
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
ACV sweet spot          <$2k        $15k–$200k  $5k–$50k    $1k–$20k
Buyer = User?           Yes         No          Mixed        Mixed
Sales cycle             Days        30–180d     30–90d       60–120d
Required CAC ratio      <3×LTV      <3×LTV      <3×LTV      <5×LTV
Time-to-value           <10 min     Days–weeks  Days         Weeks
Core GTM hire           Growth eng  AE + SDR    Content + SA Devrel/CM
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Decision rule:
1. ACV < $3k AND buyer = user → PLG first
2. ACV > $10k OR buyer ≠ user → SLG required
3. High-research category, long cycle → layer in Marketing-Led
4. Developer or practitioner audience → add Community-Led

SaasFlow situation:
  ACV = $8,400 | Buyer = VP Ops | User = Ops analyst
  → Primary motion: SLG  |  Secondary: Marketing-Led
  → PLG trial for product-qualified lead (PQL) signal only

Unit Economics That Drive Motion Choice

The right motion is inseparable from the numbers. Two ratios govern this decision:

  • LTV:CAC ratio — target ≥ 3:1. If your CAC is $5k and LTV is $25k, SLG is viable. If CAC is $2k and LTV is $8k, you need a lower-touch motion.
  • Payback period — months to recover CAC from gross margin. SLG typically 12–18 months; PLG 6–12 months. Series A investors want <18 months.

TAM / SAM / SOM — The Framework Investors Actually Use

Most founders inflate TAM. Investors fund SAM and SOM.

  • TAM (Total Addressable Market): Everyone who could theoretically buy your product. Useful for category sizing only.
  • SAM (Serviceable Addressable Market): The portion of TAM you can realistically reach with your current distribution. This is the fundraising number.
  • SOM (Serviceable Obtainable Market): What you can capture in 2–3 years given your GTM capacity. This is your operating plan number.

What PMF Actually Means — and How to Measure It

Product-market fit is not a feeling. It is a measurable state where your product solves a real problem for a repeatable segment.

The three most reliable PMF signals:

  • Retention curve flattening: D30 or M3 retention stabilizes above 20% for B2B. If the curve keeps declining, you have no PMF regardless of NPS.
  • Sean Ellis Test: "How disappointed would you be if you could no longer use this product?" — 40%+ "Very disappointed" is the PMF threshold.
  • Revenue from pull, not push: Customers find you, not just through outbound. Organic sign-ups or inbound demo requests appear without paid spend.

The Three Layers of ICP

Most ICP definitions stop at firmographics. That produces a list of 10,000 companies that all look the same. Add technographic and psychographic layers to build a list that converts.

  • Firmographic: Company size, industry, geography, funding stage, revenue range. The "who."
  • Technographic: What tools they already use. A company using Salesforce + HubSpot + Zapier signals GTM maturity and budget. The "how they buy."
  • Psychographic: Growth stage, hiring patterns, content consumption, community activity. The "why now."

Value Proposition vs. Positioning

These are not the same thing, and conflating them creates generic messaging.

  • Value proposition: The specific outcome your product delivers, quantified. "SaasFlow reduces ops team time spent on manual reporting from 12 hours/week to 2 hours/week."
  • Positioning: How you occupy a distinct place in the buyer's mind relative to alternatives. "For ops teams who've outgrown Zapier, SaasFlow is the ops automation platform built for Revenue Operations workflows."

Why Sequences Beat Single Touches

60% of deals that close required 5+ touches before the first reply. Single-email campaigns are not sequences; they are broadcasts. A sequence earns attention through pattern and persistence.

Three principles of effective sequences:

  • Channel diversity: Email + LinkedIn + phone creates redundancy. If email goes to spam, LinkedIn lands. If LinkedIn is ignored, the phone call breaks the pattern.
  • Value escalation: Each touch adds something new — a case study, a stat, a question, an insight — rather than re-asking the same thing.
  • Timing cadence: Day 1, 3, 7, 10, 14, 21, 30. Front-loaded for freshness; spaced for patience.

Why a Single Provider Fails

No single data provider covers 100% of your ICP. Apollo covers 70% of US contacts. LinkedIn is authoritative but rate-limited. Using a single provider leaves 30–40% of your list unenriched. A waterfall solves this by cascading through providers until a match is found.

Real-world benchmark: single provider email match rate ≈ 40–60%; 3-provider waterfall ≈ 75–85%.

The Personalization Stack

AI personalization is not "change the first name." It is using enriched data to generate context-specific language that a human SDR would spend 5 minutes researching and writing per prospect — done in milliseconds at scale.

  • Level 3 — AI-generated: Use an LLM to synthesize enriched context into a custom sentence or paragraph. Requires good prompt design and enriched inputs.

        non-sycophantic cold email openers. No greetings. No emojis.
        Maximum 2 sentences.

User:   "Write a personalized email opener for {{contact_name}},
         {{contact_title}} at {{company_name}}.

         Context:
         - Recent news: {{recent_news_summary}}
         - Ops pain signal: {{ops_pain_signal}}
         - Hiring signal: {{hiring_ops_signal}}
         - Company size: {{employee_count}} employees

         The opener must reference ONE specific context signal (news,
         hiring, or pain) and bridge it to the cost of manual ops work.
         Do not mention SaasFlow. Do not use 'I hope this finds you well.'"

Example output:
  "Saw {company} is hiring a RevOps Analyst — usually means the team
   is underwater with manual work before a new hire can help. That's
   the gap we typically see costing ops teams 10+ hours per week."

System: You write follow-up emails for B2B SaaS. Short, direct,

User:   "Write a 3-sentence follow-up email for {{contact_name}}

         First email referenced: {{previous_email_summary}}.
         New angle: use this customer result — {{reference_case_study}}.
         End with a soft CTA (question or low-friction ask)."

PROMPT 4: Objection Handler (for reply "Not the right time")
System: You handle objections in B2B sales emails. Never argue.
        Acknowledge, reframe, leave the door open. 2–3 sentences max.

User:   "{{contact_name}} replied: '{{objection_text}}'.
         Company context: {{company_name}}, {{recent_news}}.
         Write a reply that: (1) acknowledges their timing, (2) offers
         one piece of value they can use RIGHT NOW without buying anything,
         (3) asks if 90 days from now would be a better time."

Exercise 4.3 — SaasFlow AI Personalization Pipeline

Scenario: SaasFlow will send 500 personalized emails next Monday using AI-generated openers from the prompt library above.

  1. Map the full data pipeline: what enrichment columns must exist before the AI personalization step runs?
  2. Write a quality control check: how do you sample 10% of AI-generated openers before sending, and what criteria make you reject a batch?
  3. Design the A/B test: Variant A = AI-generated opener (Prompt 1), Variant B = human-written template from Module 2.3. What sample size, what success metric, and what duration?
  4. Write a "fallback prompt" for Prompt 1: what should the AI generate when no context signals are available (no news, no pain signal, no hiring signal)?
  5. Propose how you would measure the incremental lift from AI personalization vs. variable substitution alone — what experiment design answers this?
