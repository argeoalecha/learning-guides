How to use this guide — you do NOT have to start at Module 1.1
This guide is built to be non-linear. Each module is self-contained with its own prerequisites, skip-check, and outcomes. Use the Placement Quiz below to find your entry point, or jump straight to any module using the Module Index. Every exercise and capstone uses one running scenario — FitForge, a high-ticket $3,000 12-week fitness-transformation offer — so you practice the whole sales motion on a single business. See the FitForge brief in the shared datasets folder — there's no data file, just the fixed scenario facts every script and exercise builds on.

Phase 1: Foundations & Mindset

Weeks 1–2 | Goal: Adopt the service-first sales mindset and understand why people actually buy

Module 1.1 — The Sales Mindset & The Game

Level: Beginner · Prereqs: None · Time: ~3h
Skip-check (test-out): Explain in writing why selling is a service done FOR the buyer, and why volume + a strong offer beats "convincing."
Skip if: you already sell from a service mindset and don't take "no" personally.
Unlocks: every later module.

Selling Is a Service, Not a Manipulation

If you truly believe your offer changes lives, NOT selling it is the unethical act. The job is to help a prospect make a decision that is good for them — which often means helping them past their own hesitation. Pressure that benefits you is manipulation; conviction that benefits them is service.

The Iron Rule & Volume

Hormozi's iron rule: the more you do, the less any single outcome matters. A closer who needs the sale loses it. A closer with 40 more conversations this week is calm, curious, and detached from any one result — and closes more because of it.

THE GAME (mental model)
- You don't need a YES from this person. You need the truth.
- "No" is data, not rejection. Find out WHY, then decide if you can help.
- Detachment from outcome = the highest-status posture in the room.
- More volume → more skill → more calm → higher close rate. Compounding.

Belief Comes First

You cannot transfer a belief you do not hold. Before any call, you must believe three things: the problem is real, your solution works, and this person can succeed with it. If you can't, fix the offer or disqualify the prospect — don't fake it.

Exercise 1.1 — Mindset Brief

Scenario: FitForge ($3,000 12-week transformation)

  1. Write one paragraph: why is selling FitForge a service to the right person?
  2. List 3 beliefs you must hold before a FitForge sales call
  3. Reframe three "no" responses as data ("too expensive" → what is it really telling you?)
  4. Write the volume math: if your close rate is 20%, how many calls to land 5 clients?

Module 1.2 — Why People Buy — Value & Emotion

Level: Beginner · Prereqs: 1.1 · Time: ~4h
Skip-check (test-out): For FitForge, write the emotional driver behind the purchase and the logical justification the buyer will tell others.
Skip if: you already sell to emotion and confirm with logic.
Unlocks: 2.1, 4.1.

Emotion Buys, Logic Justifies

People buy with emotion and rationalize with logic. Nobody buys a fitness program for "12 sessions and a meal plan" — they buy confidence, attraction, energy, and relief from shame. Sell the felt outcome; provide the logic as the permission slip.

Pain → Dream → Bridge

THE BUYING JOURNEY
Current Pain  →  Dream Outcome  →  Your Offer (the bridge)

- Make the current pain conscious and specific (cost of inaction).
- Make the dream outcome vivid and believable.
- Position the offer as the shortest, most certain bridge between them.

Status & Identity

Most purchases are a vote for a future identity: "the kind of person who is fit / runs a real business / is in control." Speak to who they want to become, not just what they want to have. Selling features invites comparison; selling identity creates belonging — and belonging is stickier than any feature list.

  2. Write the logical justification the buyer gives their spouse
  3. Describe the cost of inaction in vivid, specific terms
  4. Write one sentence selling the future identity, not the features

Module 1.3 — Know Your Prospect

Level: Beginner · Prereqs: 1.1 · Time: ~5h
Skip-check (test-out): Produce a one-page avatar for the FitForge buyer: dream outcome, top pains, objections, and where they hang out.
Skip if: you can already describe your buyer's dream outcome and fears in their words.
Unlocks: 2.1, 3.1.

The Avatar

You sell to one person, not a market. Define them precisely enough that you could pick them out of a crowd and finish their sentences.

FITFORGE AVATAR — "Busy Brian," 38
Dream outcome : Lose 20 lbs, feel strong, fit into old clothes, energy for kids
Top pains     : Tried and failed before; no time; embarrassed at the gym
Deep fear     : "I'll waste money and quit again like every other time"
Buys when     : A doctor's warning, a photo, a milestone birthday
Hangs out     : Instagram fitness reels, r/loseit, local gyms, dad groups

Voice of Customer

Harvest the exact words prospects use from reviews, DMs, and calls. Selling in their language ("I want to stop feeling winded on the stairs") beats your jargon ("improve cardiovascular capacity") every time.

Phase 2: The Grand Slam Offer

Weeks 3–5 | Goal: Build an offer so good people feel stupid saying no

Module 2.1 — The Value Equation

Level: Beginner–Intermediate · Prereqs: 1.3 · Time: ~6h
Skip-check (test-out): Score FitForge on all four value variables and name one concrete change that raises perceived value.
Skip if: you can recite and apply all four variables of the Value Equation.
Unlocks: 2.2, 2.3, Capstone 2.

The Equation

Hormozi's Value Equation is the engine of every great offer. You raise value by increasing the top and shrinking the bottom.

            Dream Outcome  ×  Perceived Likelihood of Achievement
VALUE  =  ────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
              Time Delay   ×   Effort & Sacrifice

Increase value by:
  ↑ Dream Outcome      — bigger, more vivid result
  ↑ Likelihood         — proof, guarantees, track record, reversals
  ↓ Time Delay         — faster first win, quicker total result
  ↓ Effort & Sacrifice — done-for-you, simpler, less willpower required

The Highest Leverage: Likelihood & Effort

Most sellers only push "dream outcome" louder. The fastest value gains usually come from raising perceived likelihood (proof + guarantees) and lowering effort (do it for them). "Lose 20 lbs" is common; "lose 20 lbs without giving up the foods you love, or it's free" is a Grand Slam.

Exercise 2.1 — Score & Lift

Scenario: FitForge

Retrieval practice: before step 2, write the four Value Equation variable names from memory — which two are in the numerator and which two in the denominator? Draft your answer first, then check the framework above.

  1. Write FitForge's current dream outcome in one vivid sentence
  2. Score each of the 4 variables from 1–10 as the offer stands today
  3. Propose one concrete change to raise likelihood (proof/guarantee)
  4. Propose one concrete change to lower effort & sacrifice
  5. Propose one change to shorten time-to-first-win

Module 2.2 — Pricing & Premium Positioning

Level: Intermediate · Prereqs: 2.1 · Time: ~5h
Skip-check (test-out): Justify FitForge's $3,000 price on value-to-the-buyer and write the ROI framing you'd say on a call.
Skip if: you price on value delivered and can defend a premium without flinching.
Unlocks: 2.3, Capstone 2.

Charge More

Low prices attract the worst clients and starve you of the margin needed to over-deliver. A higher price raises perceived value, increases commitment (and therefore results), and funds a better experience. Price is a positioning decision, not a cost calculation.

Anchor to Value, Not Cost

PRICE FRAMING (say it as ROI, not expense)
"What is being 20 lbs lighter and pain-free worth over the next 10 years?"
"You'll spend more than $3,000 on failed programs, supplements, and
 takeout in the next year alone — this replaces all of it with a result."

Rule: never defend price. Re-anchor to the dream outcome and the cost of NOT solving it.

  2. Write a cost-of-inaction tally (money already wasted, health cost)
  3. Draft a 3-sentence ROI re-anchor you'd say when someone says "that's a lot"
  4. Decide on a payment-plan option and how you'd present it

Module 2.3 — Enhancing the Offer

Level: Intermediate · Prereqs: 2.1 · Time: ~6h
Skip-check (test-out): Add a stack of bonuses, a strong guarantee, and honest scarcity/urgency to the FitForge offer.
Skip if: you deliberately use guarantees, bonuses, scarcity, urgency, and naming.
Unlocks: Capstone 2.

Bonuses & The Stack

Solve each objection with a bonus. Present them one at a time so perceived value stacks far above the price before you ever name the number.

Guarantees (Reverse the Risk)

GUARANTEE TYPES
- Unconditional : "Full refund within 30 days, no questions."
- Conditional   : "Do the work and don't lose 15 lbs in 12 weeks? Train free until you do."
- Anti-guarantee: "All sales final" (for premium, high-demand offers)
A strong conditional guarantee raises perceived likelihood AND filters for committed buyers.

Scarcity, Urgency & Naming

Scarcity (limited spots) and urgency (a real deadline) move buyers from "someday" to "now" — but only if honest. Manufactured scarcity that a prospect can see through destroys the trust built across the whole call and loses the sale permanently. A clear, benefit-rich name ("The 12-Week Dad-Bod Reversal") sells before you say a word.

  3. Add one honest scarcity element and one honest urgency element
  4. Name the offer with a benefit-driven title

Phase 3: Getting Leads

Weeks 6–8 | Goal: Create a reliable flow of engaged leads using the Core Four

Module 3.1 — Leads & Lead Magnets

Level: Beginner–Intermediate · Prereqs: 1.3 · Time: ~5h
Skip-check (test-out): Design a FitForge lead magnet that gives a real, complete win and naturally leads to the paid offer.
Skip if: you know what makes an engaged lead and can build a lead magnet that converts.
Unlocks: 3.2, 3.3, Capstone 3.

Lead vs. Engaged Lead

A lead is anyone you can contact. An engaged lead has shown interest — replied, opted in, booked. You don't have a traffic problem; you have an engagement problem. Everything in lead-gen exists to turn contacts into engaged conversations.

The Lead Magnet

GREAT LEAD MAGNET = solves ONE narrow problem completely, fast, and for free.
It should:
  1. Reveal a problem they didn't know they had (or quantify it).
  2. Deliver a real, complete win — not a teaser.
  3. Make the paid offer the obvious next step.

FitForge example: "The 7-Day Dad Reset" — a free week of done-for-you
meals + 10-min workouts. A real win that makes the 12 weeks the obvious next step.

Module 3.2 — The Core Four

Level: Intermediate · Prereqs: 3.1 · Time: ~10h
Skip-check (test-out): Write a daily action plan for FitForge across all four lead-gen channels with specific volume targets.
Skip if: you can name the Core Four and already run at least two consistently.
Unlocks: 3.3, Capstone 3. The engine of lead generation.

The Four Ways to Get Anyone's Attention

THE CORE FOUR (warm/cold × one-to-one/one-to-many)

                 ONE-TO-ONE              ONE-TO-MANY
WARM    Warm Outreach              Posting Content
(know you)  (DM/text/call people    (social posts, video,
             who know you)           email list)

COLD    Cold Outreach             Paid Ads
(strangers) (cold DM/email/call)    (Meta/Google/YouTube ads)

Warm Outreach & Content First

Beginners with no audience and no budget start with warm outreach (people who already know you) and content (so the warm audience grows). Cold outreach and paid ads scale later once the offer and messaging are proven. Starting with warm channels validates your message without spending money — you learn what resonates before paying to amplify it.

Give Before You Ask

WARM OUTREACH SCRIPT (FitForge)
"Hey [Name] — I'm taking on a few people for a 12-week transformation
and thought of you. No pitch: I put together a free 7-day reset that's
helped a bunch of busy dads drop the first few pounds. Want me to send it?"

→ Lead with value (the free reset), not the offer. The conversation is the win.

Module 3.3 — Referrals & Lead Getters

Level: Intermediate · Prereqs: 3.2 · Time: ~5h
Skip-check (test-out): Design a referral ask + incentive for FitForge that clients will actually act on.
Skip if: you have a referral system and understand "lead getters" (affiliates, employees, agencies).

Referrals: The Cheapest, Best Leads

Referred buyers close faster, pay more, and churn less. Don't wait for them — engineer them. Ask at the moment of peak happiness (a client's first big win), make the ask specific, and give both sides a reason.

REFERRAL ASK (at a client's first win)
"Amazing — down 6 lbs in two weeks! Quick favor: who's the ONE friend
you wish would do this with you? I'll give them the same fast start I gave
you, and knock $300 off your next block as a thank-you."

Lead Getters

Beyond doing the four yourself, you can recruit others to get leads for you: affiliates, agencies, employees, and partners. This is how lead-gen scales past your own hours.

Module 3.4 — Landing Pages That Convert

Level: Intermediate · Prereqs: 3.1 · Time: ~8h
Skip-check (test-out): Wireframe and write a FitForge opt-in page and offer page that turn clicks into booked calls.
Skip if: you design conversion-focused, mobile-first pages with one promise, one form, and one CTA.
Unlocks: Capstone 3. This is where traffic becomes engaged leads.

The Core Four sends people somewhere. That "somewhere" — your landing page — is where a click becomes a lead. A great offer on a confusing page still fails. The job of the page is exactly one thing: get the visitor to take the next step (opt in for the lead magnet, or book a call).

One Page, One Goal

A landing page is not a website. Strip everything that isn't the goal: no top navigation, no "About," no competing links. Every element either moves the visitor toward the single call-to-action or it's removed. Confusion kills conversion.

OPT-IN (LEAD CAPTURE) PAGE — above-the-fold anatomy
┌───────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│  HEADLINE: the promise (dream outcome + time)  │
│  Subhead: who it's for + the objection killed  │
│                                                │
│  [ visual / before-after / short VSL ]         │
│                                                │
│  • benefit bullet 1   • benefit bullet 2       │
│  • benefit bullet 3                            │
│                                                │
│  [  email field  ] [  GET THE FREE RESET  ]    │  ← ONE form, ONE CTA
│  ★★★★★ "Down 6 lbs in 2 weeks" — real proof    │
└───────────────────────────────────────────────┘
No nav bar. No other links. One decision.

Conversion Copy

The headline does 80% of the work. Use the same dream outcome and objection language you collected in Module 1.3 — the visitor should feel the page was written for them.

HEADLINE FORMULA
[Dream outcome] + [time frame] + [objection removed]

FitForge opt-in : "Drop your first 7 lbs in 7 days — without the gym
                   or giving up the food you love."
FitForge offer  : "The 12-Week Dad-Bod Reversal: get strong and lean
                   in 12 weeks, or train free until you do."

Then: 3 benefit bullets (outcomes, not features) · proof · ONE CTA repeated.

Design Principles & Mobile-First

Most of your traffic arrives on a phone, often on a slow connection — so design for mobile first: a single column, a thumb-reachable button, big tap targets, and a fast-loading page (compress images, minimal scripts). Use visual hierarchy (the eye should land on headline → CTA), a high-contrast button color, and generous whitespace.

<!-- Minimal, mobile-first opt-in hero (semantic + one form) -->
<section class="optin">
  <h1>Drop your first 7 lbs in 7 days</h1>
  <p class="sub">For busy dads — no gym, no giving up the food you love.</p>
  <ul>
    <li>Done-for-you meals &amp; 10-minute workouts</li>
    <li>A real result in week one</li>
    <li>Zero guesswork</li>
  </ul>
  <form action="/subscribe" method="post">
    <input type="email" name="email" placeholder="you@email.com" required>
    <button type="submit">Get the free 7-day reset</button>
  </form>
  <p class="proof">★★★★★ "Down 6 lbs in two weeks" — Brian, 38</p>
</section>

Measure & Improve

The one metric that matters here is conversion rate — visitors ÷ opt-ins (or ÷ booked calls). Improve it by testing ONE thing at a time (headline, CTA text, hero image). A page going from 20% to 30% opt-in is a 50% lift in leads from the same traffic.

Phase 4: Closing — The CLOSER Framework

Weeks 9–12 | Goal: Run a structured, low-pressure sales call that consistently closes

Module 4.1 — The CLOSER Framework

Level: Beginner–Intermediate · Prereqs: 1.2 · Time: ~5h
Skip-check (test-out): Recite what each CLOSER letter means and map a FitForge call to the six steps.
Skip if: CLOSER is already your default call structure.
Unlocks: 4.2, 4.3. The backbone of the sales call.

What CLOSER Stands For

C — Clarify why they're there (get them to say the reason out loud)
L — Label the problem (name what's really going on)
O — Overview their past pain (what they've tried, why it failed)
S — Sell the vacation (the outcome/destination — not the mechanism)
E — Explain away concerns (handle objections)
R — Reinforce the decision (lock in the buy; prevent buyer's remorse)

The Shape of a Great Call

The first three letters (C-L-O) are diagnosis: you spend most of the call asking questions and listening. Only after the prospect has stated their problem and pain do you present (S), handle concerns (E), and close + reinforce (R). Most failed calls pitch too early.

Exercise 4.1 — Map the Call

Scenario: FitForge

  1. Write what each CLOSER letter means in your own words
  2. Draft one opening question for the "Clarify" step
  3. Mark which steps are "ask & listen" vs "present & ask"
  4. Estimate how much of the call should be the prospect talking

Module 4.2 — Discovery & Diagnosis (C·L·O)

Level: Intermediate · Prereqs: 4.1 · Time: ~8h
Skip-check (test-out): Write a question bank that takes a FitForge prospect through Clarify, Label, and Overview.
Skip if: you run discovery with questions and let the prospect state their own pain.
Unlocks: 4.3, Capstone 4.

Ask, Don't Tell

The prospect must convince themselves. Your questions guide them to say the problem, the stakes, and the cost of inaction — in their own voice. What they say, they believe; what you say, they doubt.

DISCOVERY QUESTION BANK (FitForge)
Clarify : "What made you book this call today, specifically?"
          "Why now and not six months ago?"
Label   : "So if I'm hearing you right, the real issue is ___ — is that fair?"
Overview: "What have you already tried?"  "Why do you think it didn't stick?"
          "What's it costing you to stay where you are — health, confidence, energy?"

Active Listening & The Pause

Reflect their words back, then go quiet. Silence after a question is pressure that works FOR the prospect — it gives them room to reach a real answer. Don't rescue the silence.

Exercise 4.2 — Discovery Script

Scenario: FitForge

  1. Write 3 "Clarify" questions and 3 "Overview" questions
  2. Write a "Label" reflection that names the prospect's real problem
  3. Write a cost-of-inaction question that surfaces the stakes
  4. Mark two places to deliberately pause and stay silent

Module 4.3 — Presenting & Closing (S·E·R)

Level: Intermediate · Prereqs: 4.2 · Time: ~8h
Skip-check (test-out): Write the present-and-close section of a FitForge call: sell the vacation, handle one concern, ask, and reinforce.
Skip if: you present to the stated problem, ask directly, and reinforce after the yes.
Unlocks: 5.1, Capstone 4.

Sell the Vacation, Not the Plane Flight

Prospects don't want your mechanism (the workouts, the app, the check-ins) — they want the destination. Present only the parts of your offer that map directly to the problem THEY stated, framed as the outcome.

PRESENT (tie every feature to their stated dream)
"You said you want to feel strong and stop being winded with your kids.
 Here's exactly how we get you there in 12 weeks..." [outcome, then mechanism]

THE ASK (direct, then silent)
"It's $3,000 for the full 12 weeks, and based on everything you told me,
 you're exactly who this works for. Do you want to get started?"
[then STOP talking]

Reinforce the Decision

The moment after "yes" is when doubt creeps in. Congratulate them, restate the outcome they just bought, and tell them the immediate next step. Reinforcement prevents refunds and cancellations.

Phase 5: Objections, Follow-Up & Scaling

Weeks 13–15 | Goal: Handle resistance, follow up relentlessly, and turn your selling into a system

Module 5.1 — Handling Objections

Level: Intermediate · Prereqs: 4.3 · Time: ~8h
Skip-check (test-out): Write calm, non-defensive responses to the four core objections for FitForge.
Skip if: you can handle price, time, belief, and decision objections without pressure.
Unlocks: 5.3, Capstone 5.

The Four Core Objections

Almost every objection is one of four (or a stall hiding one):
  1. MONEY    — "I can't afford it" / "too expensive"
  2. TIME     — "I'm too busy right now"
  3. BELIEF   — "Will this actually work for ME?"
  4. DECISION — "I need to think / ask my spouse"  (often fear in disguise)

Common mistake: Trying to "overcome" or "defeat" an objection. An objection is information, not an attack — the goal is to understand what is really being said, not to win an argument. Arguing creates resistance; genuine curiosity dissolves it.

Acknowledge → Reframe → Re-ask

OBJECTION PATTERN (never argue)
Acknowledge: "Totally fair, and I appreciate you being honest."
Reframe:     re-anchor to the dream outcome / cost of inaction.
Re-ask:      a calm question that moves forward.

Example — "Too expensive":
"Fair. Can I ask — is it that the money isn't there, or that you're not
 yet sure it'll work? Because those are two very different problems."
(Money → payment plan. Belief → proof + guarantee.)

Most Objections Are Failures Earlier in the Call

A "price" objection usually means value wasn't built in discovery; a "think about it" usually means the problem wasn't made urgent. The best objection handling happens in C·L·O — before the objection exists.

Exercise 5.1 — Objection Playbook

Scenario: FitForge

  1. Write an Acknowledge → Reframe → Re-ask response for each of the four objections
  2. Write the diagnostic question that splits "money" from "belief"
  3. For "I need to ask my spouse," write a response that invites the spouse in
  4. For each objection, note which earlier CLOSER step should have prevented it

Module 5.2 — Follow-Up & Persistence

Level: Intermediate · Prereqs: 4.1 · Time: ~4h
Skip-check (test-out): Write a multi-touch follow-up sequence for a FitForge prospect who said "not right now."
Skip if: you run a written follow-up sequence and don't quit after one "no."
Unlocks: Capstone 5.

The Fortune Is in the Follow-Up

Most sales happen after the first conversation, yet most sellers stop after one touch. A "no" is usually "not yet." Persistent, value-led follow-up — not nagging — wins the deals everyone else abandons.

FOLLOW-UP SEQUENCE (after "not right now")
Day 1  : Recap value + the outcome they wanted. "Door's open when you're ready."
Day 3  : Send a relevant win/case study (proof).
Day 7  : New angle — address the real hesitation you suspect.
Day 14 : A reason to act now (a spot opening, a deadline) — if honest.
Day 30 : Pure value, no ask. Stay top of mind.

Module 5.3 — Building a Sales System & Team

Level: Intermediate · Prereqs: 5.1 · Time: ~8h

Track What Matters

CORE SALES METRICS
  Leads → Booked Calls → Show Rate → Close Rate → Cash Collected
  - Close rate      = sales ÷ calls taken
  - Cash collected  = what actually hit the account (not "contract value")
  - Cost per lead, cost per acquisition (when running ads)
You can only improve what you measure. Find the weakest link and fix that first.

From Operator to System

To scale past yourself: document the offer, the script, the objection playbook, and the follow-up; record great calls as training; track every closer's numbers; and coach to the metric. The goal is a process that produces results without depending on your personal charisma.

Glossary

Canonical terms for this guide. Definitions state what a term is, not how to use it. Each Avoid line lists synonyms that cause confusion.

Foundations & Mindset

Dream Outcome
The specific, vivid future state a prospect wants — health, wealth, relationships, or status — stated in their own words, not your marketing language.
Avoid: "desired result," "goal," "benefit" — these are too generic to anchor a sale.
Cost of Inaction
The cumulative price a prospect pays — in money, health, time, or identity — for staying where they are instead of solving the problem today.
Avoid: "consequence," "downside" — these sound like threats; cost of inaction is a calculation, not a warning.
Emotional Driver
The underlying feeling that initiates a purchase decision before logic enters — confidence, relief, identity, fear of missing out. The emotion buys; logic only justifies.
Avoid: "pain point," "motivation" — pain point is one type of driver; motivation is too broad.
Voice of Customer (VoC)
The exact phrases prospects use to describe their problem and dream outcome, harvested from reviews, DMs, and calls. Selling in VoC language always outperforms your internal jargon.
Avoid: "customer research," "survey data" — VoC is the verbatim language, not a summary of themes.
Service Mindset
The belief that selling is something you do FOR a qualified prospect — helping them past hesitation that harms them — not something you do TO them to hit a quota.
Avoid: "positive attitude," "consultative selling" — those describe behaviors; service mindset is the foundational belief that unlocks them.

Offer Building

Value Equation
Hormozi's formula for perceived value: (Dream Outcome × Perceived Likelihood of Achievement) ÷ (Time Delay × Effort & Sacrifice). You raise value by increasing the numerator and shrinking the denominator.
Avoid: "value proposition," "value ladder" — those are different frameworks; the Value Equation is this specific formula.
Grand Slam Offer
An offer so good the prospect feels stupid saying no — constructed by maximizing all four Value Equation variables, then stacking bonuses, a strong guarantee, and honest scarcity or urgency.
Avoid: "irresistible offer," "premium offer" — Grand Slam Offer is the specific Hormozi framework, not a generic adjective.
Guarantee
A risk-reversal mechanism that shifts the downside of failure from the buyer to the seller, raising perceived likelihood. A conditional guarantee ("do the work and get the result, or we keep working for free") also filters for committed buyers.
Avoid: "refund policy," "money-back guarantee" — a guarantee is a selling tool that raises perceived value, not just a return policy.
Bonus Stack
A sequence of additions to the core offer, presented one at a time, each designed to kill one specific objection before it surfaces. The stack inflates perceived value far above the price before you name the number.
Avoid: "add-ons," "extras," "perks" — the stack is a deliberate objection-prevention sequence, not a list of nice-to-haves.
Scarcity
A real, honest limit on supply — available spots, production capacity, or physical inventory — that moves prospects from "someday" to "now." Must be factually true; manufactured scarcity permanently destroys trust.
Avoid: "fake urgency," "pressure tactic" — these describe dishonest use; scarcity itself is legitimate when real.
Urgency
A real deadline or time-bound reason to act — a price increase date, a cohort start, a limited-time bonus. Urgency limits time; scarcity limits quantity. Both require honesty.
Avoid: using "urgency" and "scarcity" interchangeably — they are distinct levers targeting different decision barriers.

Lead Generation

Lead
Anyone you can contact — an email address, a phone number, a DM handle. A lead has not yet shown intent; they are a contact, not a prospect.
Avoid: using "lead" and "prospect" interchangeably — a prospect has demonstrated interest; a lead has not necessarily.
Engaged Lead
A lead who has shown active interest — replied to outreach, opted in for a lead magnet, or booked a call. Engaged leads are the only leads that matter; the goal of all lead generation is to create them.
Avoid: "warm lead" — ambiguous; "engaged lead" is the precise term Hormozi uses.
Lead Magnet
A free, complete solution to one narrow problem that delivers a real win fast and makes the paid offer the obvious next step. It must give a full result, not a teaser or a partial solution.
Avoid: "freebie," "opt-in bribe," "content upgrade" — those describe the exchange mechanic; lead magnet describes the value it must deliver.
Core Four
Hormozi's four lead-generation channels organized as a 2×2: warm outreach, posting content, cold outreach, and paid ads — warm/cold on one axis, one-to-one/one-to-many on the other.
Avoid: "the four channels," "lead sources" — Core Four is the specific Hormozi taxonomy with the 2×2 structure.
Warm Outreach
Personally contacting people who already know you — friends, followers, past clients — to offer value or start a conversation. It is one-to-one and warm, making it the highest-converting starting channel for beginners.
Avoid: "referral outreach," "warm prospecting" — warm outreach is a specific Core Four channel, not a catch-all for non-cold contact.
Lead Getter
A person or system that generates leads on your behalf — an affiliate, a referral partner, an employee, or an agency. Lead getters let you scale lead flow beyond your personal hours.
Avoid: "referral partner," "affiliate" — those are types of lead getter; use the specific type when precision matters.

Closing & Objections

CLOSER
Hormozi's six-step call structure: Clarify why they are there, Label the problem, Overview past pain, Sell the vacation (outcome), Explain away concerns, Reinforce the decision. C·L·O is diagnostic; S·E·R is presentation and close.
Avoid: "CLOSER script" — CLOSER is a call structure, not a word-for-word transcript. A script keeps you in your head; the structure frees you to listen.
Sell the Vacation
The principle of presenting the destination the prospect wants — the outcome — rather than the mechanism (how the program works). Prospects buy the result; the mechanism is only relevant as proof it works.
Avoid: "feature-benefit selling," "solution selling" — these describe presenting what you offer; Sell the Vacation means presenting the outcome as the primary object of desire.
Objection
Resistance to moving forward — almost always one of four types: money, time, belief, or decision (often fear in disguise). An objection is information about what is unresolved, not a rejection.
Avoid: "pushback," "concern" — concern is softer and blurs the diagnostic precision; objection names the thing you must resolve.
Reinforce
The final CLOSER step — immediately after the yes — in which you congratulate the buyer, restate the outcome they just purchased, and give them the next concrete step. Skipping Reinforce is the primary source of buyer's remorse, refunds, and cancellations.
Avoid: "post-close," "onboarding intro" — those describe logistics; Reinforce is a specific psychological step that locks in the buying decision.
Acknowledge–Reframe–Re-ask
The three-move objection pattern: validate the concern without defending (Acknowledge), re-anchor to the dream outcome or cost of inaction (Reframe), then ask one forward-moving question (Re-ask). The goal is understanding, not argument.
Avoid: "objection rebuttal," "handling script" — a rebuttal is a counterargument; this pattern is a diagnostic and re-anchoring sequence.
